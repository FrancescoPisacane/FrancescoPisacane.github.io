I design the control systems that keep space telescopes perfectly still.

I'm Francesco, a mechanical engineering master's student at Politecnico di Milano. I did my thesis at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, working on active wavefront control for the mirror of a future space observatory.

About

I'm drawn to the place where mechanics, control and real hardware meet. My bachelor's in Automation and Control Engineering taught me the theory; my master's, on the mechatronics and robotics track, is where I learned to make it work on a bench.

My thesis lives on IRIS, a testbed connected to NASA's Habitable Worlds Observatory. I work on piezoelectric actuators, telescope control elements and the rigid body control system that holds a segmented mirror in alignment, down to the nanometre scale.

Away from the lab I build things with Arduino, shoot photos and drone video, and walk up mountains whenever I can.

Projects

Research first, then the projects I built with classmates and on my own.

Active wavefront control for a segmented space telescope

Master's thesis · NASA JPL

A control pipeline for the rigid body control system of a segmented mirror on the IRIS testbed. It combines hysteresis and creep compensation for piezoelectric actuators with model-based control, so the mirror holds its shape despite the actuators' nonlinear behaviour.

  • RBCS
  • Piezoelectric actuators
  • Hysteresis compensation
  • MPC
  • MATLAB / Simulink

Rotary inverted pendulum

University group project

Dynamic model, swing-up and stabilisation of a Furuta pendulum, with a comparison between the model and the physical setup.

  • Nonlinear dynamics
  • State feedback
  • Lab hardware

Manta, a biomimetic underwater robot

University group project

A manta-ray inspired swimmer: CAD of the flapping fins and the control strategy that drives them.

  • CAD
  • Bio-inspired robotics
  • Control

Autonomous vehicles with ROS 2
